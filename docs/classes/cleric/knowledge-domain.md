# Knowledge Domain

*Cleric Divine Domain — Player's Handbook, p. 59*

> A scholar-priest who reads minds, borrows skills, and glimpses the history of any object.

The gods of knowledge value learning and understanding above all. Some teach that knowledge is to be gathered and shared in libraries and universities; others that it should be kept secret, doled out only to the worthy.

**How it plays.** The strongest out-of-combat utility domain, weak in a straight fight. Read Thoughts and Visions of the Past are investigation powerhouses.

## Features

### Blessings of Knowledge

*1st level*

You learn two languages of your choice. You also become proficient in two skills of your choice from Arcana, History, Nature, and Religion — and your proficiency bonus is doubled for any ability check using either of those skills.

### Channel Divinity: Knowledge of the Ages

*2nd level*

As an action, you choose one skill or tool. For 10 minutes, you have proficiency with that skill or tool.

### Channel Divinity: Read Thoughts

*6th level*

As an action, choose one creature within 60 feet. It must make a Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, you can read its surface thoughts for 1 minute, gaining insight into its emotions and foremost concerns. As an action during that time, you can end the effect and cast *suggestion* on the creature without expending a slot — it automatically fails its save against that casting.

### Potent Spellcasting

*8th level*

You add your Wisdom modifier to the damage you deal with any cleric cantrip.

### Visions of the Past

*17th level*

After at least 1 minute of meditation, you can call up visions of the past relating to an object you hold or your immediate surroundings. *Object Reading* shows you the object's previous owners; *Area Reading* shows significant events that occurred within 50 feet in the past number of days equal to your Wisdom modifier. Each minute of concentration costs you one minute of the total; once you use the feature, you can't use it again until you finish a short or long rest.
